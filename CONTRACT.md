# Product contract

Target edition: 17 October 2026. Lanyard is a complete US hourly-workforce product: build and fill schedules, handle swaps and call-outs, capture actual work, control labor cost and overtime, allocate tips, and reconcile approved hours with payroll. A buyer of Deputy, When I Work, Homebase or 7shifts can move this whole operational job to Lanyard; Connecteam and Legion set additional mobile and optimization depth. The first buyer runs a 10–100 person US service, retail or hospitality business. Currency and budgets are USD; sites use US time zones, daylight saving and employer-reviewed federal/state/local policy.

This contract is the product destination, not a claim that every target ships today. The Status section records the code and test evidence. Existing guarantees remain part of the full job; the old Australian single-venue framing and exclusions of swaps, tips, pay-cost calculation, multi-site work and live provider submission no longer define the target. This round changes documentation only. Provider access, agreements, app distribution and policy engineering remain delivery work.

## Existing guarantees

- **ACCESS-001 — Native sessions and server-side authority.** Owners grant access; managers make staffing and time decisions; staff see their own private work. Every private page, state read, mutation and payroll download checks the live native session, role and ownership on the server. Browser mutations require the configured origin. Deactivation revokes existing staff access immediately; a manager cannot approve their own time or leave. **Acceptance evidence:** Sign in as owner, manager and two staff through native invitation/confirmation routes; use the app and HTTP commands, attempt cross-person reads, self-approval, signed-out and cross-origin mutations, then deactivate a signed-in employee. Unauthorized attempts fail without writes.

- **PEOPLE-001 — Staff lifecycle and payroll identities.** The owner adds, edits, activates, invites and deactivates employees with a unique email and payroll identifier and an explicit staff or manager role; staff without email use PEOPLE-003. Stale revisions cannot overwrite newer people records. Invitation and activation remain separate actions; removing access retains employment history. **Acceptance evidence:** In People, create and invite staff, reject duplicate email/payroll IDs, edit the exact revision and deactivate a signed-in person. Their prior shifts and hours remain while their private requests are denied.

- **MOVE-001 — Reviewed Staff CSV move-in.** The owner previews Staff CSV v1 without writes and applies only the exact reviewed fingerprint. Imported people start inactive and uninvited. Original inputs, mapping provenance, results and an import journal remain inspectable; undo removes untouched imports and preserves later native edits and dependent work. This documented format is not a verified vendor adapter. **Acceptance evidence:** Paste Staff CSV in Move in, preview and inspect it, reject changed bytes at apply, apply the reviewed input, read its originals/journal, edit one imported person and undo. Only untouched rows disappear and nobody is invited automatically.

- **ROSTER-001 — Availability and explicit leave decisions.** Staff record availability and request dated time off; managers approve or reject the exact pending request. Pending requests do not silently become approved absence. Self-approval and approval that collides with an already published shift are refused; the manager resolves coverage explicitly. **Acceptance evidence:** Through staff and manager app/command doors record availability, request overlapping leave, reject self-approval, approve a valid request and attempt a published-shift conflict. Inspect the decision author and retained state.

- **ROSTER-002 — Conflict-checked revision publication.** Managers or delegated agents prepare a weekly roster, then a human manager publishes the exact revision. Publication rechecks active staff, availability, approved leave and overlapping assignments atomically. Republishing supersedes the previous week revision without deleting its work history; stale drafts fail. **Acceptance evidence:** Prepare an invalid week through the roster API and see named conflicts and refused publication. Prepare a valid week, attempt stale approval, publish as a manager and inspect superseded records and activity. A changed input requires fresh review.

- **ROSTER-003 — Personal published schedule confirmation.** Employees see their own current published shifts and confirm the exact shift revision. A confirmation records who and when, and cannot acknowledge a superseded shift or somebody else's assignment. Confirmation indicates the employee's response, not completed work. Seeing coworkers' shifts is ROSTER-004. **Acceptance evidence:** Publish a roster, open it as each employee and confirm a personal shift. Attempt a foreign or stale confirmation; inspect the visible published assignment and confirmation separately from clock records.

- **TIME-001 — Identity-bound phone capture and replay.** A signed-in employee clocks in and out with original capture time, receipt time and a stable command identity. Retrying the same body/person returns the same result without duplicate time; reusing its identity for a changed body or person fails. Only one open clock is allowed and captured unpaid break minutes reduce worked minutes. The present page retry facility requires the browser to remain open; durable offline capture is CLOCK-002. **Acceptance evidence:** Use staff clock commands to start and stop a shift, replay both events, and reuse an identity with another time/person. Read one closed clock and one submitted timesheet with correct net minutes; an employee cannot approve it.

- **TIME-002 — Exact time approval and linked corrections.** Managers review submitted hours, prepare reasoned missed time and approve the exact revision. A correction links to the latest approved source, retains the original and requires fresh approval; overlap and future-time checks apply. Employees never approve their own work and a pending correction prevents exporting its obsolete source. Approving many timesheets at once is TIME-008, under the same exact-revision rule. **Acceptance evidence:** Prepare missed time, approve it through a manager session, correct its break or duration, approve the correction and inspect retained superseded history. Reject self/stale approval and overlapping work; attempt payroll export while a correction awaits decision.

- **PAYROLL-001 — Reviewed once-only payroll files and signed adjustments.** A manager reviews the exact approved-source CSV and explicitly exports that revision. Unique source claims prevent two batches claiming the same time revision. Downloads verify immutable bytes and hash and are manager-only; later corrections export the signed difference from previously claimed time. Payroll IDs must still match at export. Downloading twice does not create a new batch, but cannot prevent somebody uploading a file twice outside Lanyard. **Acceptance evidence:** Prepare two competing batches through payroll commands, export one and reject the other. Download identical bytes twice through the authenticated CSV route, refuse staff access, then approve a negative correction and export only its delta while the old download stays unchanged.

- **PAYROLL-002 — Explicit manual provider acknowledgement.** After downloading a payroll file, a manager records the provider reference against the exact exported batch. Lanyard labels this as a person-recorded acknowledgement. It does not imply a live transfer, provider readback, payroll application or wage payment; those states belong to SYNC-001 and the named connectors. **Acceptance evidence:** Export hours, record a provider reference through payroll.acknowledge, reload the batch and inspect its reference, actor and time. A draft cannot be acknowledged and a stale batch revision is refused.

- **AGENT-001 — Preparation-only delegated authority.** The owner issues expiring revocable preparation keys. Agents read a reduced context and prepare roster, time and payroll drafts; only a native human manager publishes, approves or exports. Agents receive no payroll IDs, download files or staff contact details through their context. Server authority applies through both human and agent doors. **Acceptance evidence:** Issue a key, read agent context and prepare time; attempt approval/export through both HTTP doors, approve as a human, revoke the key and immediately retry. Inspect redacted context and distinct agent preparation versus human decisions.

- **ARCHIVE-001 — Complete installation archive with authority exclusions.** Owners export and atomically restore supported people, original owner identity, rosters, time revisions, payroll files/source claims, events and import originals/journals into an empty compatible installation. Corrupt or populated imports fail. Sessions, sign-in links, agent keys, provider credentials and the development outbox do not travel; configuration is re-entered and the archived owner signs in again. This retained-owner policy deliberately replaces a generic Base restore that might establish a new owner. A destination or supplied later erasure journal fences older erased identities; disconnected old copies cannot know about later erasure. **Acceptance evidence:** Use public rer export/import commands on an installation with staff, published shifts, corrected/exported hours and import history. Round-trip records and files, reject damaged/populated imports, inspect excluded authority and sign in anew as the archived owner; supply a later erasure journal to refuse resurrection.

- **ERASE-001 — Unused identity erasure and restore fence.** An owner erases only an unused, uninvited, never-imported staff identity, replacing personal values and recording an erasure fence that rejects recreation/import by the erased identity. Employed, invited or imported people are deactivated and their legally relevant history retained; expanded retention and privacy controls are PRIVACY-001. **Acceptance evidence:** Create an unused person, erase via its exact revision, inspect the state for removed name/email, refuse native/CSV recreation and refuse an older archive with the later fence. Attempt erasing invited or imported staff and require retention with deactivation available.

- **AUDIT-001 — Durable decisions and transactional history.** Publication, time decisions, payroll export/acknowledgement, import actions, erasure and payroll downloads retain actor, role, subject and time. State mutation, payroll source claims, decision event and replay outcome commit together or fail together. An import action (apply, resume, undo or rollback) records one event per staff record it creates or removes, named for the action and committed in the same atomic batch as that record; a payroll download records its own event. Audit survives restart and supported archives. Activity is read by role, newest 100 events per reader: owners read all of it; a manager reads the events about people whose home cafe is the manager's own (an unnamed cafe is one cafe) and about that cafe's settings, with a setting naming no cafe read by every manager, until TEAM-001 adds finer site and team scopes; staff read the events on their own records (their staff record and records carrying their staff id, never rosters, batches, postings or templates covering several people); everyone reads the events they performed; events on anonymous FEEDBACK-001 feedback record their actor only as a keyed digest and are read by no one; an employee never reads events on a private JOURNAL-001 note about them; agents read none. An event's people are its subject record's person and the people on a roster's or posting's shifts, a template's slots and a payroll batch's lines, judged by today's home cafe; installation settings and records no longer present are owner-only. An event shows only actor, role, action, subject id and time, never employee-private fields. **Acceptance evidence:** Perform the roster-to-payroll journey through public doors, inspect each actor/revision/event, restart and round-trip the archive. Cause a stale concurrent mutation and verify no orphan decision or source claim appears. Read the activity as the owner (all of it), the cafe's manager (everything about the cafe's people, nothing about another cafe), a second cafe's manager (only that cafe's person and setting) and an employee (only events on their own records, never roster publication or payroll); each event carries only actor, role, action, subject id and time.

- **PUBLIC-001 — Public run site and installed Lobby.** The public run site presents the target product, opens /run for installation and builds owner guides at /docs from repository Markdown. The installed / remains the shared Lobby, reflecting the durable owner claim before separate first-run confirmation. Public marketing, installation and private operational state stay distinguishable; enabling deployment remains an explicit repository operation. **Acceptance evidence:** Build the run site, follow homepage to /run and /docs, then claim an installation through native setup and inspect the installed Lobby before and after first-run confirmation. Private product data remains session-protected.

- **BASE-001 — Maintained installation and operations commands.** The maintained command surface remains SEED-COMMAND-CONTRACT.md: pinned dependencies, setup, dev/preview, build/check/verify, owner-account deploy, export/import, diagnostics and explicit reset. Product-owned archive and erasure policy replaces only the stated Base data behavior; native auth, deployment identities and shared Lobby are retained. Build/deploy never run tests and development credentials cannot enter hosted output. **Acceptance evidence:** From a fresh clone use documented command help and structural check, launch and sign in, build and verify, exercise backup/restore, then perform a provider-authorized deployment and health readback. Reset requires explicit confirmation and never removes another installation.

## Target clauses

### Workforce records and schedule building

- **TEAM-001 — Multi-location roles and delegated management.** Owners organize businesses, locations, departments and jobs; assign employees to multiple eligible sites; and scope schedulers, site managers, time approvers, payroll administrators and read-only analysts independently. Wage and tip data are restricted. Cross-location staff see permitted assignments while managers cannot act beyond their sites; owners can revoke grants immediately. Owners can also define custom access levels under ACCESS-003. Bulk timesheet approval (TIME-008) then covers only the approver's sites. **Acceptance evidence:** Create two locations with a shared employee and separate managers through organization settings. Use each role to schedule, approve, report and export; reject foreign-site actions and wage reads, then revoke a grant during an active session. A site manager's bulk approval pinned to a foreign-site timesheet skips it (TIME-008).

- **PEOPLE-002 — Jobs qualifications preferences and effective pay rates.** Managers maintain job skills, certifications with expiry, site eligibility, employee preferences, minimum/maximum hours, employment classification and effective-dated USD wage/job rates. Qualification expiry blocks unsuitable assignment and private wage access is restricted. Changes preserve the rate/policy basis used for past costs and payroll preparation. Applying a SCHEDULE-002 template, copy or recurring shift rechecks qualifications. **Acceptance evidence:** Add a cross-trained employee with expiring certification, different job rates and preferences; schedule across the expiry/rate boundary, reject an unqualified shift and inspect historical cost snapshots after a rate change. Apply a SCHEDULE-002 template that places an unqualified person and see the qualification conflict before publishing.

- **PEOPLE-003 — Phone-number staff accounts and text invitations.** Owners and managers add hourly staff with only a US mobile number, invite them by SMS through Twilio and let them sign in with a one-time text code bound to that number; email is optional for staff. Mobile numbers are unique among active people, verified before use and changed only through a re-verified flow. Carrier opt-out (STOP), number change or a lost phone revokes text sign-in without deleting employment history, and PEOPLE-001 payroll identity rules still apply. **Acceptance evidence:** Add a staff member with only a mobile number, send the text invitation, sign in with the code and confirm a published shift. Reject a duplicate number, an expired or replayed code and a sign-in after STOP or a number change; the person's prior shifts and hours remain and each attempt appears in audit.

- **CONTACT-001 — Emergency contacts on the shift and employee card.** Each employee has emergency contacts (name, relationship, phone) held by the field authority chosen under PAYSYNC-001: Willow or BambooHR (HR-001), the connected payroll provider where it exposes them, or Lanyard. When Lanyard is the authority staff maintain their own contacts in the employee app. Owners and site managers whose scope covers the person's shift see the contacts read-only on the shift, live-attendance and employee cards, including from a CLOCK-004 late or no-show alert; other roles and other sites cannot read them. Every view is audited and the data follows PRIVACY-001 retention and erasure. **Acceptance evidence:** As staff add two emergency contacts; as the site manager open a no-show alert for that person and call from the card. A manager at another site and a scheduler without the permission are refused and both attempts are audited. Switch the authority to Willow, change a contact there and see the card update while a Lanyard edit is refused.

- **ACCESS-002 — Manager single sign-on and required second factor.** Owners let managers and payroll administrators sign in with Google Workspace, Microsoft Entra ID or Okta through OIDC, mapped to existing Lanyard people by verified email or an explicit subject, and can require a passkey or authenticator second factor for roles that approve time or export payroll. Removing the person in the identity provider or in Lanyard ends access at the next request; staff keep text-code or email-link sign-in. **Acceptance evidence:** Configure Google and Microsoft sign-in, sign in as a mapped manager and refuse an unmapped account. Require a passkey for the payroll role, attempt an export without it (refused) and with it, then deactivate the manager and retry during the open session.

- **ACCESS-003 — Custom access levels.** Owners define named access levels from a fixed permission catalogue (view or edit drafts, publish schedules, approve swaps and leave, approve time, edit punches, view wages and labor cost, manage tips, export or submit payroll, manage people, view reports, manage integrations) and scope each level to businesses, locations and departments. The TEAM-001 roles ship as editable defaults. A person may hold several levels at different sites; nobody can create or grant a level carrying a permission they do not hold; wage visibility and self-approval rules from ACCESS-001 still apply. Changing or revoking a level takes effect at the next request and is audited with before and after permissions. **Acceptance evidence:** Create a "Shift lead" level that can edit drafts and approve swaps but cannot publish, see wages or approve time, assign it at one site and exercise it through the app and HTTP commands; refused actions write nothing. Attempt to grant a level containing payroll export as a manager without it (refused), then remove the swap permission during an active session and see the next approval refused, with both changes in audit.

- **JOURNAL-001 — Employee notes and manager journal.** Authorized managers add dated notes to an employee's record (attendance conversations, performance, commendations) with category, author and optional attachment, and choose per note whether the employee can see it. Notes are scoped by site and role, never appear in team views, agent context or broad reports, keep prior versions when edited and follow PRIVACY-001 retention, legal hold and access requests. Until TEAM-001 adds finer site and role scopes, the authorized writers and readers are the owner, acting for admin, and the managers whose home cafe is the employee's (an unnamed cafe is one cafe); nobody keeps notes about themselves. A note has a venue date, one category (attendance conversation, performance, commendation or other), its author's name, text, and at most one attachment of up to 1 MB stored outside the records and served only to a reader of the note; it is private (managers in scope and the owner) or visible to its employee read-only, without author id or history. An edit needs the note's exact revision and keeps every earlier version with its text, visibility, category, attachment, author and time. Events on a private note are never read by its employee (AUDIT-001). Notes never reach staff team views, agent context, payroll files or reports. PRIVACY-001 applies its retention, legal holds and the employee's access export to notes, the export carrying employee-visible notes only. **Acceptance evidence:** As a site manager add a private note with an attachment and an employee-visible note to an employee; read them as a second-site manager, the employee and an agent key and see only what each may read. Edit one note and inspect its retained version, and refuse a stale edit; PRIVACY-001 carries the employee's record export and which notes it contains.

- **SCHEDULE-001 — Editable visual schedules and publication review.** Managers build day/week schedules with drag-and-drop, resize, copy and bulk assignment, seeing roles, sites, headcount, breaks, coverage gaps and live conflict/cost warnings. Drafts remain private until a reviewed publish/change diff. Undoing a draft edit cannot erase published history; mobile managers can perform the same core scheduling work. **Acceptance evidence:** Build a realistic service week in the schedule UI, move and bulk-edit shifts, inspect warnings and the employee-facing change diff, publish it and verify employee views. Repeat core edits at 390px and reject stale publication.

- **SCHEDULE-002 — Reusable templates recurring shifts and duplication.** Managers save schedules and role-based shifts as templates, duplicate selected days/weeks, and generate recurring assignments with explicit end dates and exceptions. Applying a template rechecks current leave, availability, qualifications, budgets and policies without auto-publishing or copying stale confirmations. Owners and managers save, copy and repeat. Each makes a new draft revision of the target week holding its current rows (its open draft, else its published revision) plus the added shifts at the same venue wall times, so a copy across a daylight-saving change keeps 7:00 am; nothing is published and no confirmation is carried. Templates and copies keep each shift's person, times, meal minutes, label and cafe; a person no longer active is left out and named. A recurring shift repeats on chosen weekdays between an explicit first and last date and skips listed dates; adding a week's recurring shifts places only that week's occurrences. Each draft rechecks availability, approved time off and overlaps as any prepared week does; qualifications are PEOPLE-002's, budget warnings COST-001's and policy checks POLICY-001's to add to that recheck, and position-only template rows are COVER-002's open shifts. **Acceptance evidence:** Save a week, duplicate it into a holiday week and apply a recurring pattern with an exception. Review conflicts for an employee now on leave, edit only one occurrence and publish after approval without altering previous weeks.

- **SCHEDULE-003 — Cross-site coverage and double-booking prevention.** One schedule spans multiple locations and time zones with location/role coverage requirements. Employee conflicts, travel time, minimum rest, overtime and hours budgets are calculated across all their sites. Authorized managers coordinate transfers without competing bookings; site-local times, overnight shifts and US daylight saving transitions remain explicit. **Acceptance evidence:** Build schedules for two US sites sharing staff, including an overnight and DST-boundary shift. Try overlapping and impossible-travel assignments, compare cross-site hours and publish a valid coverage plan through authorized managers.

- **SCHEDULE-004 — Shift notes tasks and work-area segments.** Managers attach operational notes, checklists and job/area segments within shifts. Employees see relevant instructions on mobile, confirm completed tasks and report a blocker with attachments; segment hours map to jobs for labor and payroll without double-counting the enclosing shift. Structured notes never appear on SCHEDULE-005 printed copies or the wall display. **Acceptance evidence:** Publish a shift split between counter and kitchen with a closing checklist. Staff open it on mobile, complete tasks and report a blocker; managers inspect handover and reconcile segment totals to worked time. A printed copy and the wall display of that week show none of the notes (SCHEDULE-005).

- **LEAVE-001 — PTO balances and staffing-aware time off.** Employees view PTO/sick balances, request full/partial-day leave and see decisions; managers review coverage and policy limits before approving. Accruals, carryover and paid/unpaid codes are employer-configured with effective dates. Each leave type has one explicit authority under PAYSYNC-001: Lanyard, an HR-001 source or the connected payroll provider (PAYSYNC-004); approvals update availability and payroll classification without duplicate balances. **Acceptance evidence:** Configure a US PTO policy, accrue a balance, request partial-day leave and approve with a coverage warning. Carry a year-end balance and reconcile paid/unpaid hours; connect HR or payroll-provider authority and reject conflicting double decisions and balance edits.

- **AVAIL-001 — Recurring availability preferences and approval.** Employees set recurring weekly availability as available, preferred or unavailable blocks per day with effective start and end dates, add one-off exceptions and state preferred and maximum weekly hours. Managers may require approval of availability changes; a pending change does not alter scheduling until approved, and a change that collides with published shifts lists the affected shifts. Scheduling, swaps, offers, optimization and FAIR-001 read the effective pattern for each date. An approved pattern governs each venue date from its first day to its optional last day, the newest first day winning; a date's one-off exceptions replace that weekday's blocks. A shift touching an unavailable block or exception is flagged as marked unavailable; otherwise it is available when the effective available or preferred blocks, or dated availability windows, cover it end to end. A prepared week past the person's maximum weekly hours flags their last shift; preferred hours are recorded and shown. Owners and managers choose whether staff changes wait for approval; a pattern a manager enters for someone is effective at once, and nobody decides their own. Saving or approving lists the published shifts from its first day that it would flag. The call-out shortlist (COVERAGE-001) ranks teammates whose effective pattern prefers the whole shift first; COVER-001, COVER-003, OPT-001 and FAIR-001 carry their own reads of the pattern. **Acceptance evidence:** As staff submit a recurring pattern starting next month with one exception date, approve it as manager and schedule across the effective date. Shifts before the date follow the old pattern, a shift in an unavailable block is flagged, a preferred block ranks first in the call-out shortlist (COVER-003 keeps that order) and a still-pending change leaves conflicts unchanged.

- **LEAVE-002 — Time-off blackout dates limits and team leave calendar.** Managers set blackout dates and per-site or per-role limits on how many people may be off at once, and see a team leave calendar of approved and pending time off beside the schedule. Requests inside a blackout or over a limit are refused or routed for explicit override with a reason; employees see the allowed dates before requesting, and pending requests show distinctly from approved leave. Until TEAM-001 brings site-scoped managers, owners and managers set the rules for every cafe. A rule names a cafe, a position, both or neither (blank covers everyone). Blackout dates are inclusive venue dates, and a request touching any of them conflicts. A limit counts other covered people's approved leave on each venue date a request touches and is full when that count reaches the limit. Each rule either refuses a conflicting request or accepts it flagged for a manager; approving any conflicting request needs an override reason, recorded with the conflicts, the approver and the time. Staff see the blackout and full dates that cover them from today on, never who is off; the team leave calendar is for owners and managers and lists approved and pending leave by day of the shown week. **Acceptance evidence:** Configure a holiday blackout and a two-person limit for servers, submit three overlapping server requests and one inside the blackout, approve two, override the third with a reason and inspect the calendar and refusals as staff and as manager.

- **LEAVE-003 — Paid sick and safe leave jurisdiction presets.** Lanyard ships maintained paid sick and safe leave presets for California, Colorado, Illinois (Paid Leave for All Workers Act), New York State, Oregon, Washington, New York City, Chicago, Seattle, San Francisco, Los Angeles and Philadelphia. Each preset records accrual on hours worked, caps, carryover, waiting period, usage increment, permitted reasons and balance fields with its effective date and cited source. Accrual runs on approved worked hours at each employee's work location; balances flow to LEAVE-001 and pay codes to PAY-001. Any other jurisdiction is labeled employer-configured, unmaintained. **Acceptance evidence:** For each listed preset accrue leave from approved hours for an employee working there, cross a cap and a year boundary, take a partial-day absence and export the balance and pay code. An employee splitting time between two jurisdictions accrues under each site's preset, and an unlisted city shows the unmaintained label.

- **MINOR-001 — Minor work-hour limits and permits.** Employers record an employee's date of birth and any state work permit. Maintained presets apply federal FLSA child-labor limits for 14- and 15-year-olds (school-day, school-week, non-school-week and evening limits) and state minor rules for California, New York, Illinois, Washington, Oregon and Colorado, with configurable school calendars. Scheduling, swaps, pickups and clocking warn or refuse before a minor exceeds a limit; the age is visible only to people who need it. **Acceptance evidence:** Schedule a 15-year-old past the school-day limit and after the evening cutoff, offer them an open shift that would breach the weekly limit and attempt an early clock-in; each is refused or flagged with its rule. A 17-year-old under a state rule is checked separately, and a teammate cannot see either age.

- **SCHEDULE-005 — Printable and posted schedules.** Managers print or export the published week as PDF by site, role or employee and show a read-only wall schedule on a site screen, each stamped with the revision and publication time. Posting records support POLICY-002 posting duties, and a later change marks printed copies superseded in the app. Private notes, wages and leave reasons never print. Owners and managers print. A printed copy is a PDF built from the rows recorded at printing (person, position, shift times and shift label; never pay, contact details, notes or time-off reasons), so it downloads byte-identical later, stamped with its posting time. Its revision is the week's publication number: 1 for the first publication of that week, 2 for the next. A copy is superseded once the week's current published schedule, read with the same cafe, position or person, differs from what it printed; when a newer publication caused that, the copy shows that publication's time. The wall display is a read-only page for one cafe's current week, opened on the site screen in an owner or manager session, and reloads every minute. Private shift notes are SCHEDULE-004's to keep out and posting duties are POLICY-002's. **Acceptance evidence:** Publish a week, print site and employee PDFs, open the wall display, then change a shift. The PDFs carry the old revision, the display moves to the new revision and the posting record shows both times; wages and time-off reasons are absent from every output (SCHEDULE-004 keeps its private notes out).

- **SCHEDULE-006 — Schedule change history and revision compare.** Managers and affected employees see each shift's history: who created, moved, reassigned, swapped, cancelled or republished it, when, why, and which notices and acknowledgements followed. Managers compare any two published revisions of a week as a diff by person, site and hours, and export the change log for POLICY-002 records. **Acceptance evidence:** Publish a week, then move one shift, approve a swap and cancel another. Open each shift's history as manager and as the affected employees, compare the first and latest week revisions and export the change log with reasons and notice states.

### Coverage employee experience and follow-up

- **COVER-001 — Employee shift swaps with final approval.** Employees offer a shift or propose an exchange; eligible coworkers consent and a scoped manager approves the exact exchange. Availability, qualification, rest, overtime, leave and coverage are rechecked at decision time. Pending swaps do not change responsibility; concurrent requests cannot assign the same shift twice. Swap eligibility reads each person's effective AVAIL-001 pattern for the swapped dates. **Acceptance evidence:** On employee mobile accounts propose and accept a two-person swap, approve as manager and inspect both changed schedules/notices. Refuse an ineligible coworker, stale request and two competing approvals; retain the original assignee until completion. A coworker whose AVAIL-001 pattern marks the swapped shift unavailable is refused.

- **COVER-002 — Open shifts offers and pickup.** Managers publish unassigned shifts to eligible staff or a controlled pool. Employees see job/site/rate details and request pickup; manager policy determines approved pickup versus explicit final approval. Atomic assignment closes all other offers, rechecks eligibility and prevents duplicate pickup or silent coverage loss. A SCHEDULE-002 template row saved for a position without a person becomes an open shift when the template is applied. **Acceptance evidence:** Post an open shift, view it as eligible and ineligible staff and submit competing pickup requests. Approve one, verify all other offers close and current assignments/notices update exactly once. Apply a template with a position-only row and see it posted as an open shift.

- **COVER-003 — Call-out replacement and verified coverage.** Employees report an absence or managers record a call-out against a shift with minimal private reason disclosure. The manager sees a qualified available replacement shortlist, cost/overtime effects and timed offers; employees accept and a manager approves assignment. An unfilled shift escalates to the responsible manager and remains uncovered until accepted, then attendance is monitored through actual clock-in. Its replacement order keeps AVAIL-001's ranking: teammates whose effective pattern prefers the whole shift come first. **Acceptance evidence:** Call out before a shift, contact eligible replacements, let one offer expire, approve an accepted replacement and observe notice/acknowledgement and clock-in. A nonresponsive replacement reopens an actionable coverage exception without fabricating attendance. A teammate whose AVAIL-001 pattern prefers the shift is contacted first.

- **COVER-004 — Offer order and access-to-hours priority.** Managers choose how open shifts and replacements are offered: to all eligible staff at once, in timed tiers by priority (part-time staff who want more hours, preferred availability, fewest hours this week, seniority or a custom ranking) or first-come pickup. Where a POLICY-002 preset requires offering hours to existing staff before hiring or using temporary workers (San Jose, Seattle, San Francisco and New York City), the offers and responses are kept as the compliance record. **Acceptance evidence:** Post an open shift with a two-tier offer, let tier one expire with one decline, have tier two accept and approve. Inspect the offer log, times and responses; at a San Jose site, hiring a new person for those hours is flagged until existing part-time staff have been offered them.

- **ROSTER-004 — Team schedule visibility for staff.** Employees see who else works their site, role and day on the published schedule, showing coworker name, role and shift times only, so they can arrange swaps and handovers. Managers choose per site whether staff see the whole site or only their role, and can hide a person for safety reasons. Drafts, wages, leave reasons, contact details and other sites stay hidden. By default each site shows staff its whole published team; an owner or manager may narrow a site to the viewer's own position. A staff member's site is their home cafe on People; a shift belongs to the cafe it was scheduled at, else its person's home cafe, and an installation without cafe names is one site. Role means the person's position on People. The team view lists every currently published shift at that site (never a draft or superseded revision), each as coworker name, position, start and end only. A hidden person appears in no staff team view at any site, their own view included, staff never learn who is hidden, and the hidden person's own schedule is unchanged. Each site's setting is saved as an exact revision. **Acceptance evidence:** Publish a week for two roles at two sites; as staff open the team view and see coworkers' names and times for the permitted scope, then switch the site to role-only and hide one person. Requests for drafts, wages, contact details or another site's shifts return nothing.

- **NOTICE-001 — Delivered schedule notices and acknowledgement.** Publication, changes, pickups, swaps and cancellations create recipient-specific in-app, push, email and US SMS notices in the recipient's language (LANG-001), using APNs/FCM, Resend and Twilio as supported delivery services. Deduplicated attempts expose queued, provider-accepted, delivered where observable, failed and employee-acknowledged states separately. Inspect receipts, a provider rejection, opted-out/quiet-hour routing and duplicate callbacks; delivery alone cannot mark employee acknowledgement.

- **NOTICE-002 — Reminders escalation and payroll follow-up.** Managers set shift reminders, acknowledgement deadlines, open-offer expiry, late/no-show and missed-punch/break alerts, timesheet review cutoffs and payroll review reminders. Durable scheduled work survives restart, deduplicates per shift/revision/period, stops obsolete notices after resolution, respects employee contact settings and escalates unresolved exceptions to an accountable manager. **Acceptance evidence:** Configure a shift and payroll period, receive a reminder, leave a schedule change unacknowledged, miss a punch and cross the review deadline. Restart the scheduler, resolve each item and inspect one appropriate escalation with no obsolete follow-up.

- **CHAT-001 — Team messaging and operational handover.** Staff and authorized managers use direct, group, site and shift threads, announcements and read acknowledgements, with attachments, search and retained handover context. Deactivated people lose access; private HR/payroll facts stay outside broad team threads. Shift-related conversations link to the actual schedule revision and cannot replace a formal swap or time approval. **Acceptance evidence:** Send a site announcement and shift handover, acknowledge and search them as staff, then transfer/deactivate a member and verify thread permissions. A chat request to swap still requires the COVER-001 approval journey.

- **CAL-001 — Google and Outlook calendar subscriptions.** Employees subscribe to private revocable iCalendar feeds in Google Calendar and Outlook Calendar. Published assignments, changes and cancellations use stable event identities and correct site time zones; private notes, coworkers and draft shifts are excluded. The app states provider-controlled refresh and does not present a subscription as immediate two-way scheduling. **Acceptance evidence:** Subscribe from Google and Outlook, publish/change/cancel an overnight shift and inspect refreshed events and timezone. Rotate/revoke the feed, confirm old access fails and verify another employee cannot retrieve it.

- **FEEDBACK-001 — Post-shift employee feedback.** After clock-out employees may rate the shift, leave an optional comment or flag an issue (understaffed, equipment, safety, conflict). Managers see feedback by site, shift and manager with trends, reply and mark it resolved; where the employer enables it, an employee may hide their name from the site manager. Feedback never affects pay, approval or scheduling rank. Today feedback answers one phone-clocked shift, once, from its timesheet: a 1–5 rating, a comment, any of the four flags, or a skip; the shift's cafe is its matched shift's cafe, else the person's home cafe, and its manager is whoever published that shift's week. The owner reads every cafe's feedback and a manager their home cafe's, until TEAM-001's site scopes; skips are kept only for their author. The weekly report is the Time screen's week: response count, flags by kind and the average rating against the previous week, filtered by cafe and manager. A reply is read by the author; a manager or the owner marks it resolved. The owner, acting for the employer, turns anonymous feedback on for the installation (off until then); an anonymous answer, safety flags included, shows managers and the owner its cafe, date, manager and content only, never its author, shift or timesheet. Its author, clock, history actor and command digest are stored only as HMAC-SHA256 digests keyed by the installation secret, so its records, history and the installation archive alone never name the author; inside the installation the keys serve the author's own view and replies and the one-answer-per-shift fence, and a restore into another installation keeps the answer with no author. Events on it name no one and are read by no one (AUDIT-001). Feedback lives in its own records: timesheets, approvals, payroll, schedules, the call-out shortlist and agent context never read it, and FAIR-001 and OPT-001 carry that in their text and acceptance before they ship. **Acceptance evidence:** Clock out three employees and submit a low rating with a safety flag, an anonymous comment and a skip. The site manager sees the flag, resolves it with a reply the employee sees and the weekly report shows the trend; the anonymous author stays hidden, including from the stored records and history, which link it only through keyed digests, and the rating is absent from every ranking input.

- **LOG-001 — Manager logbook and shift handover log.** Managers keep a daily logbook per site with entries by category (sales notes, incidents, maintenance, staffing, menu outages), attachments and mentions, read by the next shift's managers with read receipts. Entries link to shifts, people and attendance exceptions; corrections are new versions, and an incident exports for insurance or for HR follow-up in Willow. **Acceptance evidence:** Write closing and incident entries with a photo at one site, mention the opening manager, read and acknowledge as that manager, search last month by category and export the incident. A manager of another site cannot see it and an edit retains the original.

- **MOBILE-001 — Employee and manager mobile apps.** iOS and Android apps plus accessible responsive web cover schedules, team view, availability, leave, swaps, pickups, call-outs, messaging, time capture, earnings and acknowledgements, in English and Spanish (LANG-001); manager approvals and live attendance on mobile are MOBILE-002. Push deep-links to authorized current records; a revoked or shared device cannot expose another employee's work. Keyboard/screen-reader flows, clear pending/error states and recovery from weak connectivity cover core tasks. **Acceptance evidence:** Use realistic multi-site data at 1440px and 390px and on supported iOS/Android devices; complete the shift-change-to-clock journey in English and in Spanish, follow a push deep link, lose connectivity and sign out/revoke. Confirm accessible focus, current data and explicit unsent state.

- **MOBILE-002 — Manager mobile approvals and live attendance.** Managers and time approvers use the mobile apps and 390px web to see live attendance (CLOCK-004), approve, edit with a reason or bulk-approve timesheets (TIME-002, TIME-008, TIME-009), decide leave, swaps, pickups and availability, and view punch photos and locations, under the same site scopes and exact-revision rules as desktop. Late, no-show, missed-punch and pending-approval pushes deep-link to the record. **Acceptance evidence:** On a phone-sized manager session open a late-arrival push, view the attendance board, approve a swap and a clean timesheet, edit one punch with a reason and bulk-approve the rest. A stale revision is refused and a foreign-site record is not visible.

- **LANG-001 — Spanish staff experience and notices.** Every staff-facing surface ships in English and US Spanish: the employee mobile apps, responsive web, shared kiosk and browser clock, schedule, swap and offer screens, timesheet review, clock questions and attestations, and every outbound push, SMS and email notice and calendar event text. Add an untranslated staff-facing string and see the check fail.

### Attendance US policy and labor economics

- **CLOCK-001 — Shared kiosk clocks and device authority.** A manager enrolls and revokes a tablet/shared-kiosk time clock limited to approved sites. Employees identify themselves with individual PIN/session verification, clock jobs and breaks without viewing others' private records, and sign out between users. Sites may require a punch photo at each kiosk punch (CLOCK-005), and the kiosk shows each signed-in employee their own language (LANG-001). Kiosk administration requires separate authority; lost devices and repeated failed PINs can be locked. Kiosk punches are rounded under the cafe's TIME-005 rule like phone punches. **Acceptance evidence:** Enroll a shared tablet with photo verification on, have two employees (one Spanish-preference) clock consecutive shifts and breaks, attempt identity guessing and cross-person reads, then revoke the tablet during a session. Inspect correctly attributed punches each carrying its photo, the language each employee saw and denied further captures. A kiosk punch at a cafe with a TIME-005 rule shows its captured and payable time.

- **CLOCK-002 — Durable offline capture and reconnect reconciliation.** Phone and kiosk clients persist encrypted pending clock/break events across page closure, app/device restart and loss of service, showing captured versus received times and pending state. Reconnection reconciles stable identities exactly once, respects revoked authority and surfaces clock skew, out-of-order or conflicting captures for review without discarding original facts. Offline punches are rounded under the TIME-005 rule in effect on their captured clock-in date. **Acceptance evidence:** Clock and break while offline, close/restart the client, reconnect and retry. Read exactly one ordered event set and corrected worked time; simulate revoked access and conflicting capture, retaining an explicit unresolved record for a manager. A reconciled offline punch is rounded under the TIME-005 rule of its captured date.

- **CLOCK-003 — Geofenced mobile clocking with privacy controls.** Employers configure per-site geofences and explicit mobile location permissions for punch-time verification, showing accuracy and reasoned exception paths when location is unavailable. Clocking location is limited to the event, with no background employee tracking; supervisors review disputed or out-of-zone punches without changing captured facts. Phone punches may also require a punch photo under CLOCK-005. Facial-recognition matching and dedicated hardware verification are optional partner integration. An out-of-zone punch records an exception on its timesheet, which TIME-008 bulk approval skips. **Acceptance evidence:** Clock inside/outside a site and with poor accuracy or location permission denied. Review a documented exception, inspect punch-time location and retention controls, then revoke device access; no off-shift location trail appears. Bulk approval (TIME-008) skips the out-of-zone timesheet with its exception named.

- **CLOCK-004 — Live attendance and late or no-show alerts.** Managers see who is scheduled, present, on break, late, missing or clocked at the wrong site, with freshness and unresolved conflicts. Scheduled shift boundaries and configured grace periods generate accountable late/no-show alerts and coverage actions; published or confirmed shifts alone never count as worked. **Acceptance evidence:** Run a service shift with an on-time, late, absent and offline employee. Compare the live attendance board to actual punches, receive a late/no-show escalation, arrange coverage and resolve only from observed or approved time facts.

- **CLOCK-005 — Punch photo verification.** Owners turn on photo capture at clock-in, clock-out and optionally break punches, per site and per device type (shared kiosk, employee phone, browser clock). The client takes a still photo at the punch and stores it with that punch event; approvers see it beside scheduled and captured times in timesheet approval (TIME-009, TIME-008) and on manager mobile. This is not face matching: no face template is created or compared, and facial-recognition sign-in remains optional partner work under BOUNDARY-001. Each employee sees a recorded notice before first capture at a site; a refused or failed camera records an explicit exception rather than silently omitting the photo. Photos are readable only by the employee and scoped approvers, never reach agents or reports, and follow PRIVACY-001 retention and erasure. A missing required photo records an exception on its timesheet, which TIME-008 bulk approval skips. **Acceptance evidence:** Turn on photos for a kiosk site and for phone clocking at a second site; clock in and out on both, then approve the timesheets and see each photo beside its punch. Deny the camera on one phone punch and see the exception; a coworker and an agent key cannot read the photos; let retention expire and erase a former employee's eligible photos while the punch facts remain. Bulk approval (TIME-008) skips the denied-camera timesheet with its exception named.

- **CLOCK-006 — Clock-in and clock-out questions and attestations.** Owners configure short questions shown at clock-in or clock-out per site or role (breaks taken, injury today, cash tips declared, or a custom yes-no or number), with required answers, Spanish text and versioned wording. Answers attach to the punch, open BREAK-001, TIME-003 or safety follow-ups when they indicate a problem and feed TIPS-001 declarations. Changing a question never rewrites earlier answers. **Acceptance evidence:** Configure a break attestation, an injury question and a cash-tips declaration; clock out answering that a meal was missed and that an injury happened. A break exception and a safety follow-up open for the manager, the declaration reaches the tip pool draft and an edited question keeps earlier answers under their old wording.

- **CLOCK-007 — Browser time clock on approved networks.** Owners enable a web time clock for back-office computers, restricted per site to approved public IP ranges or enrolled browsers, so staff can punch from a store computer without a kiosk. Punches from other networks are refused or flagged as configured, the network identity is recorded with the punch and no other device data is collected. **Acceptance evidence:** Enroll a site's IP range, clock in from it and from an outside network. The first succeeds with the network recorded, the second is refused or flagged as configured, and changing the range takes effect on the next punch.

- **CLOCK-008 — Mid-shift job and site transfers.** A clocked-in employee switches job (for example server to bartender) or, where permitted, site from phone or kiosk without clocking out. Each segment keeps its own job, site, rate and pay codes, overtime still aggregates across the day and week, and a transfer to a job the person is not qualified for is refused. **Acceptance evidence:** Clock in as server, transfer to bartender after three hours and clock out. The timesheet shows two segments with their rates and codes, the payroll export splits them, a transfer to an unqualified job is refused and the week's overtime counts both segments.

- **BREAK-001 — Timed meals breaks and exceptions.** Employees start/end paid rest and unpaid meal breaks on phone or kiosk. Configured site/state policy checks timing, duration, missed/short breaks, waivers and attestations, producing reviewable exceptions and premium-pay inputs when applicable. Manual edits preserve employee facts and approval history; missing breaks cannot silently reduce payable work. A missed or short break is recorded as an exception on its timesheet, so TIME-008 bulk approval skips that timesheet and names the exception. **Acceptance evidence:** Clock a meal and paid rest break, miss or shorten a required meal and submit an attestation. Review the exception and a reasoned correction, then reconcile net/pay-code hours and any premium input in payroll preview. A timesheet pinned for bulk approval that then gains a missed-break exception is skipped with the exception named (TIME-008).

- **BREAK-002 — Automatic meal deductions with employee attestation.** Owners may configure an automatic unpaid meal deduction per site (for example 30 minutes on shifts over 6 hours) when no meal was punched. The employee sees the deduction at clock-out and confirms the meal was taken or reports it missed or interrupted; a report reverses the deduction for approval and opens a BREAK-001 exception. Unconfirmed deductions are flagged in approval, and a site under a preset that requires recorded meal periods, such as California, cannot replace meal punches with a deduction. The rule belongs to the clock's cafe (its matched shift's cafe, else the person's home cafe) and applies when the clocked span is longer than the threshold and no meal minutes were entered; entered meal minutes replace the deduction. Confirming the meal deducts it and records the answer; reporting it missed or interrupted pays the full span with a "missed meal" or "interrupted meal" exception; no answer deducts it with an "unconfirmed meal deduction" exception. Excepted timesheets wait for a manager's single approval and TIME-008 bulk approval skips them. Jurisdiction presets that require recorded meal periods are POLICY-001's, which refuse this setting there. **Acceptance evidence:** Enable a 30-minute deduction, work a 7-hour shift without a meal punch and confirm at clock-out; work another and report the meal missed. The first deducts with the confirmation recorded, the second pays the full time with an exception, and a California site's refusal is POLICY-001's acceptance.

- **TIME-003 — Missed punches employee review and exception resolution.** Employees review daily/period timesheets, submit missing-punch or correction requests and attest to work and breaks. Managers compare schedules with captured events, investigate long/open/overlapping time and approve exact reasoned changes with employee visibility. Exceptions stay assigned and open until resolved, and deadline reminders continue under NOTICE-002. An edit changes the timesheet's revision, so a TIME-008 bulk approval pinned to the earlier revision skips it; time the employee confirms leaves TIME-008's unconfirmed state. TIME-006's flagged early and unscheduled punches open such an exception, assigned and open until resolved. **Acceptance evidence:** Miss clock-out, receive an exception, submit a correction as the employee, review/approve it as another authorized manager and inspect the original facts, attestation, reason and employee-visible outcome before payroll. A manager's edit to a timesheet another manager has pinned for bulk approval makes that approval skip it as changed (TIME-008), and the employee's confirmation moves manager-entered time out of the unconfirmed filter. A TIME-006 flagged early punch opens an exception that stays open until a manager resolves it.

- **TIME-004 — Payroll period locks and controlled reopening.** Payroll administrators define pay periods, collect employee/manager signoff and lock approved hours. Separate reopening permission requires a reason and records who reopened which period; closed or provider-applied periods accept linked late/off-cycle adjustments instead of rewriting paid history. Concurrent edits and transfers recheck period state. **Acceptance evidence:** Approve and lock a period, deny an ordinary manager's edit/reopen, reopen with payroll authority and fresh signoff, then close after provider application. A later correction produces an explicit adjustment and cannot mutate the original paid period.

- **TIME-005 — Punch rounding and snap-to-schedule.** Owners configure, per site and effective-dated, nearest-increment rounding of punches (5 minutes, a tenth of an hour or a quarter hour) or snap-to-schedule within a window, applied at capture or at approval. Rounding must be neutral: nearest-increment rounding rounds both up and down, and snap-to-schedule treats early and late arrivals and departures symmetrically, with early-arrival snapping limited to time the employee was not permitted to work under TIME-006. A rule that only reduces paid time is refused at setup. The captured time is always kept beside the payable time, both visible to the employee and approver, and a period report shows per employee the net minutes rounding added or removed. Today a site is a cafe, and a punch's cafe is its matched shift's cafe, else the person's home cafe; a rule is in effect from its venue date until a later rule for that cafe starts, and the rule in effect on the clock-in's venue date governs the punch pair and is recorded on the timesheet at clock-out. Nearest rounding moves each punch to the nearest 5-, 6- or 15-minute mark on the venue clock face, an exact half rounding up. Snap-to-schedule moves a late arrival within the window to the scheduled start and an early or late departure within the window to the scheduled end; early arrivals are never snapped, because every accepted early clock-in is permitted or approved under TIME-006; punches outside the window or without a matched shift keep their captured time. Only phone-clock time is rounded, never manual entries or corrections. A rule applied at capture sets the payable time at clock-out and one applied at approval sets it when the timesheet is approved; payable minutes (the payable span less meal minutes) are what approval, payroll export and EARN-001 pay, and the payroll file keeps the captured start and end. Only rules that round both ways save: a round-down-only or round-up-only rule is refused. Approved time is never recomputed, so a later rule never changes it. The period neutrality report is the Time screen's week: per employee, net payable minus captured minutes on that week's rounded timesheets. CLOCK-001 and CLOCK-002 round the punches they capture under the same rule, and TIME-009 and PAY-002 show and export captured beside payable time; each carries that in its text and acceptance before it ships. **Acceptance evidence:** Configure quarter-hour nearest rounding at one site and a 7-minute snap-to-schedule window at another; clock in at 8:52, 8:58, 9:04 and 9:09 at each. Approve and read captured and payable times side by side and the period neutrality report, try to save a round-down-only rule (refused) and change the rule effective next week without altering approved history.

- **TIME-006 — Early clock-in window and unscheduled punches.** Owners set per site or role how early an employee may clock in before a scheduled shift and whether unscheduled punches are allowed. A punch before the window is refused with the time it opens, or accepted and flagged for manager approval, as configured; an unscheduled punch is refused, accepted pending approval or allowed for listed roles. Flagged punches keep their captured time and open a TIME-003 exception; approved early time counts as worked. Flagged early and unscheduled punches are recorded as exceptions on their timesheets, which TIME-008 bulk approval skips. Owners set a rule per cafe and may add a position rule that replaces its cafe's rule; with no rule clock-in stays unrestricted. A clock-in's shift is the person's published shift in progress at the captured time, else their earliest published shift starting within 12 hours after it; with none the punch is unscheduled. The window is measured from that shift's start to the captured time; a refused punch writes nothing and names, in venue time, when clock-in opens. Allowing unscheduled punches for listed roles is a position rule set to allow. A flagged punch keeps its captured time and carries "early clock-in" or "unscheduled punch" on its timesheet, shown to approvers and skipped by TIME-008 bulk approval; it becomes payable only through a manager's single approval. **Acceptance evidence:** With a 10-minute window attempt a clock-in 25 minutes early (refused, showing when it opens), switch the site to flag mode and repeat (accepted and flagged), then clock in without a shift (pending approval). Approve the early time and correct the unscheduled punch with a reason; both captured times stay visible and only approved time is payable. Bulk approval (TIME-008) skips the flagged timesheets and names each exception.

- **TIME-007 — Forgotten clock-out reminder cap and provisional close.** Owners set a reminder after scheduled end plus a grace period and a maximum open-punch length per site. A still-open punch triggers a push or SMS reminder, then at the configured point (scheduled end or the cap for unscheduled work) stops accruing and is marked provisionally closed with a TIME-003 exception. A provisional end is never treated as fact: it cannot be approved or exported until the employee or a manager confirms or corrects it, and the original open state is retained. A provisional close is an exception on the timesheet, which TIME-008 bulk approval skips. **Acceptance evidence:** Clock in for a 9-to-5 shift and never clock out; receive the reminder at 5:15, see the punch provisionally closed with an open exception at the configured point and an export refused. Correct the end time as the employee, approve as a manager and export; an unscheduled punch left open reaches the 12-hour cap with the same exception. A bulk approval (TIME-008) including the provisionally closed timesheet skips it with the exception named.

- **TIME-008 — Bulk timesheet approval with pinned revisions.** Time approvers filter timesheets by site, role, period and state (clean, exception, edited, unconfirmed) and approve many in one action within their scope. The action pins each included timesheet's exact revision; at commit any item whose revision changed, that gained an exception (missed break, early or unscheduled punch, provisional close, out-of-zone or missing required photo) or that is the approver's own time is skipped, and the result lists approved and skipped items with reasons. Each approval records the same actor, time and revision as a single approval, and nothing outside the approver's sites is touched. Until TEAM-001 brings site-scoped approvers, owners and managers approve for every cafe, as a single approval does, and the cafe filter bounds the action: a pinned item outside the filter is skipped, never approved. Cafe is the person's home cafe, role is their People position, period is an inclusive range of venue dates on which the work starts, and the states are exception (capture recorded an exception), edited (it corrects earlier approved time), unconfirmed (a manager or agent entered it rather than the person's own clock) and clean (none of these); edited time is also unconfirmed. At commit a pinned item is skipped with its reason when it is missing, its revision changed, it is the approver's own time, it falls outside the filter, it carries an exception or the single approval refuses it (such as an overlap); every other item goes through the single approval and records its own time.approve decision. No capture path records an exception yet: BREAK-001 (missed or short breaks), TIME-006 (early and unscheduled punches), TIME-007 (provisional close), CLOCK-003 (out-of-zone punches) and CLOCK-005 (missing required photos) each record theirs on the timesheet, and bulk approval skips them, before they ship; edits of waiting time arrive with TIME-003 and change the revision. **Acceptance evidence:** Filter a 60-person week at one cafe to clean timesheets and bulk-approve the listed revisions while another manager decides one included timesheet. The result approves the rest and skips that one, the approver's own timesheet, a foreign-cafe timesheet and hand-entered time pinned outside the filter, each with its reason; audit shows one decision per approved revision and the skipped items are untouched. The break-exception skip is BREAK-001's acceptance and the edit skip TIME-003's.

- **TIME-009 — Timesheet approval review with schedule variance.** The approval screen shows per timesheet the scheduled, captured and payable times, variance minutes, breaks taken against required, punch photo and location where enabled, job and site, rounding effect, open exceptions, cost and the employee's running period and overtime totals, with filters for exceptions. Approvers act from the same row: approve, edit with a reason or send back to the employee. The review shows TIME-005 captured and payable time side by side. **Acceptance evidence:** Open a week where one employee arrived early, one missed a meal and one clocked out of zone. Each row shows the right variance, break, photo, location and cost; filtering to exceptions lists exactly those three, and an edit with a reason keeps the captured facts. The review shows each punch's TIME-005 captured and payable time.

- **POLICY-001 — US overtime meal and rest policy rules.** Employers configure effective-dated FLSA workweeks and state/local daily/weekly overtime, double time, consecutive-day, rest/meal and minimum-rest rules; minor limits are MINOR-001 and reporting-time, call-in, split-shift and spread-of-hours premiums are PAYRULE-003. Lanyard ships maintained presets, each versioned with its effective date and cited source, for: federal FLSA (weekly overtime over 40 hours); California (daily overtime and double time, seventh consecutive day, meal and rest periods with premium pay); Alaska (daily overtime); Nevada (daily overtime under its wage threshold, meal and rest periods); Colorado (daily and consecutive-hours overtime, meal and rest periods); Oregon (meal and rest periods); Washington (meal and rest periods); New York (meal periods); and Illinois (One Day Rest in Seven Act meal period and weekly day of rest). Any other jurisdiction or employer rule is labeled employer-configured, unmaintained on the site and in every warning it raises. Planning warns before assignment/publication; actual hours aggregate across jobs/sites and classify regular/overtime/premiums with explainable rule/version inputs. For a person working several rates, cost estimates use a weighted-average regular rate labeled as an estimate; the provider's regular-rate treatment stays authoritative. Changing rules never silently rewrites closed payroll. A preset that requires recorded meal periods, such as California, refuses BREAK-002's automatic meal deduction for its sites. Policy checks also run when a SCHEDULE-002 template, copy or recurring shift is applied. Daily and state overtime it computes replaces the 40-hour weekly overtime in the employee's EARN-001 estimate. **Acceptance evidence:** Run one scenario per maintained preset: federal 42-hour week; California 10-hour day, 13-hour day, seventh consecutive day and a late meal; Alaska 9-hour day; Nevada 9-hour day below and above the wage threshold; Colorado 13-hour day; Oregon and Washington missed meal and rest periods; New York late meal period; Illinois 7.5-hour shift without a 20-minute meal and a seventh consecutive workday. Each raises its pre-publish warning and the expected actual classification traced to the preset version, worked across two sites where it applies. An unlisted city shows the unmaintained label, and a new preset version requires reviewed recalculation of open periods. Apply the California preset to a site and see BREAK-002's automatic meal deduction refused there. Apply a SCHEDULE-002 template that breaks a state rule and see the policy warning before publishing. The employee's EARN-001 estimate uses the preset's daily and weekly overtime.

- **POLICY-002 — Predictive scheduling and fair-workweek changes.** Site-specific predictive-scheduling policies track coverage, advance notice, good-faith estimates, employee consent, schedule-change/cancellation premiums, rest between shifts, access to hours and required records. Lanyard ships maintained fair-workweek presets, each versioned with its covered industries and employer-size thresholds, rules and cited source, for: New York City (fast food and retail), Chicago, Philadelphia, Seattle, San Francisco (Formula Retail Employee Rights Ordinances), Los Angeles (retail), Oregon (statewide), Evanston, Berkeley, Emeryville and San Jose (Opportunity to Work). Any other ordinance is labeled employer-configured, unmaintained. A post-publication change previews consent/premium consequences and any employer-authorized exception; employees receive the changed revision and retain their response. Policy dependencies never silently waive an obligation. Posting duties are met from SCHEDULE-005 posting records. **Acceptance evidence:** Run one scenario per maintained preset: publish a week for a covered employer, then add, change or cancel a shift inside that preset's notice window, schedule a short rest between a closing and an opening shift where the preset limits it and offer added hours where it requires access to hours. Each produces the preset's consent request, premium input and record; an employer below the preset's threshold at the same location produces none; an unapproved exception is refused; approved premium inputs reach payroll; an unlisted city shows the unmaintained label. Inspect the SCHEDULE-005 posting record of the posted week.

- **PAYRULE-001 — Shift differentials.** Payroll administrators define effective-dated differentials by time of day, day of week, site, role or shift tag, as a USD amount per hour or a multiplier, with a stacking order. Approved hours split into differential segments, each with its pay code (PAY-001) and cost (COST-001); overtime classification follows the provider-confirmed regular-rate treatment. Differentials appear in the employee's EARN-001 estimate. **Acceptance evidence:** Configure a $1.50-per-hour night differential after 10 PM and a weekend multiplier; work an overnight Saturday shift crossing midnight. Read the split segments, codes and cost, change the rate effective next week without altering approved history and export the period with differential lines mapped. The employee's EARN-001 estimate includes the differential.

- **PAYRULE-002 — Holiday calendar and holiday premiums.** Owners keep a company holiday calendar seeded with US federal holidays and editable per site, and set holiday rules: a multiplier or added rate for hours worked on a holiday, holiday pay for eligible employees who do not work, and eligibility such as working the scheduled shifts before and after. Scheduling shows holiday cost and approved holiday hours carry their own pay codes. Holiday premiums appear in the employee's EARN-001 estimate. **Acceptance evidence:** Seed the federal calendar, add a company holiday at one site, schedule and work Thanksgiving and the company holiday, then approve. Holiday premium lines appear with codes and cost, an employee who misses the shift after the holiday is shown ineligible under the rule and the other site has no company holiday. The employee's EARN-001 estimate includes the holiday premium.

- **PAYRULE-003 — Reporting-time call-in split-shift and spread-of-hours premiums.** Jurisdiction presets and employer rules produce premium-pay inputs when an employee reports for a shift that is cancelled or cut short (California reporting-time pay, New York call-in pay), works a split shift (California) or exceeds a 10-hour spread of hours (New York), each with the triggering facts, rule version and pay code. Premiums are reviewed payroll inputs, not final pay calculations. These premiums appear in the employee's EARN-001 estimate. **Acceptance evidence:** At a California site send an employee home an hour into a scheduled 8-hour shift and schedule a split shift; at a New York site work a 7 AM to 6 PM day with a long unpaid break and cancel a shift after arrival. Each premium input appears with its fact trace and code, a manager disputes one with a reason and approved inputs export in the period. The employee's EARN-001 estimate includes the premium.

- **COST-001 — Scheduled versus actual labor cost and budgets.** Managers plan USD labor budgets by site/job/day/week and compare scheduled versus worked hours, wages, overtime, tips/premiums and configured employer burdens. Variance reports distinguish estimated, approved and provider-reconciled cost; rate-effective dates, cross-site hours and forecast sales from POS connectors or SALES-001 entry are visible. Budget warnings precede publishing and overruns trigger accountable follow-up without hiding required paid time. Budget warnings also appear when a SCHEDULE-002 template, copy or recurring shift is applied. **Acceptance evidence:** Build and work a multi-role week with rate changes, overtime and a call-out; inspect budget warnings and scheduled/actual cost by site. Correct time and reconcile the variance to approved hours and provider-applied payroll without counting an unworked shift. Apply a SCHEDULE-002 template that takes the week over budget and see the warning before publishing.

- **COST-002 — In-week overtime risk alerts from actual hours.** As punches accrue, Lanyard projects each employee's workweek and daily totals, including remaining scheduled shifts at all sites, against POLICY-001 thresholds and alerts the scheduler and the employee's manager when overtime is projected, with the USD premium estimate and qualified non-overtime substitutes for the remaining shifts. Swaps and pickups that would create overtime show it before approval. **Acceptance evidence:** Schedule an employee for 38 hours and let them clock 3 extra hours by Thursday. The scheduler receives an alert projecting 41 hours with the estimated premium and a substitute list, a pickup that adds overtime shows its cost before approval and approving a substitute clears the alert.

- **ACA-001 — ACA hours measurement for variable-hour employees.** For employers at or near Applicable Large Employer size, Lanyard tracks hours of service (worked and paid leave) per employee against the Affordable Care Act 130-hours-per-month full-time test, supports the look-back measurement method (standard measurement, administrative and stability periods) for variable-hour employees and warns schedulers when planned hours would make one full-time for the period. Hours and status export to the payroll provider or Willow for 1094-C and 1095-C filing, which Lanyard does not file. **Acceptance evidence:** Configure a 12-month measurement period, load a year of approved hours for three variable-hour employees with one averaging over 30 hours a week, inspect the computed status for the stability period and the warning for another trending towards 30, and export the hours file for the filer.

- **TIPS-001 — Tips pooling and reviewed payroll allocation.** Managers import cash/card tip inputs from POS or employee declaration, configure employer-reviewed US pool eligibility, contribution/distribution and job/hour rules, and allocate by actual approved work. Staff review distributions and discrepancies; managers approve the exact pool revision. Excluded roles, rounding, corrections, tip-credit inputs and provider pay-code mapping remain traceable and never imply Lanyard moved money. Approved tips appear as their own line in the employee's EARN-001 earnings view and estimate. **Acceptance evidence:** Import Square/Toast/Clover tip inputs, allocate a service pool across eligible worked roles, reject an excluded participant and reconcile totals/rounding. Approve a correction and transfer mapped tip lines to payroll with provider readback and employee-visible history. The employee's EARN-001 earnings view shows the approved tip line in the estimate.

- **TIPS-002 — Tip credit and minimum-wage make-up checks.** For employees paid a tipped cash wage, Lanyard checks each workweek, under the site's jurisdiction, that cash wage plus allocated or declared tips meets the applicable minimum wage and raises any shortfall as a make-up pay input. It records time on duties that do not produce tips so the employer can apply its reviewed dual-jobs treatment. Sites in jurisdictions that bar a tip credit (California, Washington, Oregon, Nevada, Minnesota, Alaska and Montana) refuse a tip-credit setup. **Acceptance evidence:** Pay a server a tipped cash wage, allocate low tips one week and normal tips the next; the first week shows a make-up input and the second none. Record side work on a non-tipped duty and see it reported, and try to enable a tip credit at a California site (refused).

- **REPORT-001 — Operational reports exports and scheduled delivery.** Authorized managers filter and export schedules, attendance/late/no-show, breaks/exceptions, PTO, worked hours, overtime, labor cost versus budget/sales, tip allocations and payroll reconciliation by site/job/person/period. - **REPORT-002 — Compliance and audit record reports.** Owners produce jurisdiction-scoped reports for audits: schedule postings and changes with notice given, consent and predictability-pay records (POLICY-002), meal and rest compliance and premiums (BREAK-001), minor-hour checks (MINOR-001), sick leave accrual and use (LEAVE-003), rounding neutrality (TIME-005), tip-credit make-up (TIPS-002) and punch edits with reasons. Produce each report for an auditor-scoped reader, reconcile counts to the source records and confirm unrelated wage and personal data are excluded.

- **REPORT-003 — Sales per labor hour and labor percentage by interval.** Managers see labor cost as a percentage of sales and sales per labor hour by site, role and 15-, 30- or 60-minute interval for scheduled, forecast and actual hours, using POS or SALES-001 data, with targets per daypart and flags where actual labor percentage exceeds target. **Acceptance evidence:** With a day of hourly sales and scheduled and actual hours at two sites, compare sales per labor hour and labor percentage by interval with hand-calculated values, set a dinner target and see the exceeded intervals; missing sales hours show as incomplete, never zero.

- **DASH-001 — Manager daily operations dashboard.** A site or multi-site manager opens one dashboard for today and this week: who is on now and late (CLOCK-004), open and unconfirmed shifts, pending approvals (time, leave, swaps, availability), overtime risk (COST-002), labor percentage against sales and budget, unresolved exceptions and the payroll cutoff countdown, each linking to its record and scoped to their sites. **Acceptance evidence:** Seed a live day with a late arrival, an open shift, three pending approvals, an overtime risk and a missed punch. The dashboard shows each with correct counts and links, a second-site manager sees only their site and resolving an item clears it.

- **EARN-001 — Employee hours and estimated earnings.** Employees see their own scheduled and worked hours this period, overtime and premiums so far, approved tips and an estimated gross pay labeled as an estimate before taxes and deductions, with the pay period and pay date. Employers can turn the estimate off; it never shows coworkers' data, and the payroll provider's pay statement stays authoritative. Today the period is the workweek, Monday to Sunday on the venue calendar, and a record counts in the week of its venue start date; scheduled hours are the person's published shifts less their meal minutes; approved time (TIME-005 payable minutes) over 40 hours in the week is overtime at 1.5x the person's hourly rate; the estimate is approved regular and overtime minutes at that rate, rounded half up to the cent; submitted time shows as pending and stays out of the estimate. Estimates are on until the owner, acting for the employer, turns them off; with estimates off or no rate on file no amount or rate is shown, and with estimates off the person's own rate is withheld from their session. The pay date is the owner's set number of days after the week ends, shown as not set until saved, and the owner's https link opens the provider's pay statement. Premiums join the estimate with PAYRULE-001, PAYRULE-002, PAYRULE-003 and POLICY-001 (daily and state overtime), approved tips with TIPS-001, and pay schedules replace the workweek with PAY-009; each carries that in its text and acceptance before it ships. **Acceptance evidence:** As an employee with regular and overtime lines mid-period open the earnings view and reconcile hours and estimate to approved records, with a coworker's approved time absent. An unapproved punch shows as pending, turning estimates off hides the amount and the view links to the provider pay statement.

The optimizer never reads FEEDBACK-001 ratings, comments or flags. **Acceptance evidence:** Generate two feasible schedule alternatives from real location demand and employee preferences, inspect costs, FAIR-001 measures and uncovered intervals, raise the fairness priority and see the FAIR-001 spread and preference gap fall with the USD cost difference reported, then approve one diff. An impossible budget/skill requirement stays explicit and cannot waive a break or qualification. An optimized week never places someone in an AVAIL-001 unavailable block or past their weekly maximum. Optimized schedules are unchanged by FEEDBACK-001 ratings and flags.

- **FAIR-001 — Named fairness measures.** Fairness in OPT-001, TRADEOFF-001 and LEARN-001 is computed from four named measures over a configurable trailing window (default eight weeks) among employees eligible for the same role and site: (1) preference gap, the absolute difference between each person's scheduled and preferred weekly hours, reported as mean and maximum; (2) undesirable-shift spread, each person's count of closing, close-then-open, weekend and holiday shifts (the employer chooses which tags count), reported as maximum minus minimum and standard deviation; (3) preference violations, the count of shifts placed in an employee's prefer-not blocks; (4) hours-offer equity, each part-time employee's share of offered extra hours against their requested share. Each measure shows its inputs and window; protected traits are never inputs. Preference measures read AVAIL-001 preferred blocks and preferred weekly hours. Fairness measures never read FEEDBACK-001 ratings, comments or flags. **Acceptance evidence:** On a fixed eight-week dataset with a skewed closing rotation, generate a schedule at a low and at a high fairness priority. The high-priority schedule's undesirable-shift spread and preference gap are no higher and, in the seeded case where a fairer feasible schedule exists, strictly lower; the USD cost difference and any coverage change between the two are reported, and each measure recomputes by hand from the exported inputs. Preference measures count shifts in AVAIL-001 preferred blocks and hours against preferred weekly hours. Measures are unchanged by FEEDBACK-001 ratings and flags.

## Push further

Agents serve the same staffing-to-payroll job. Each delegated action is source-backed, revocable and bounded by the named human decisions; an uncertain or unresolved outcome remains open.

- **COVERAGE-001 — Carry an absence or demand swing to verified coverage.** With manager-authorized contact and staffing policies, the coverage agent reads the affected shift, demand delta, qualifications, availability, leave, cross-site actual/approved hours, fatigue/rest limits, rates and prior responses. It proposes qualified available non-overtime replacements first, explains exceptions, sends authorized offers, collects employee consent, expires losing offers and brings the exact change to a human manager. It carries the approved change through schedule notices and payroll preparation, then checks acknowledgement and actual clock-in; only evidence closes coverage. Revocation stops further outreach and stale data triggers fresh review. **Acceptance evidence:** Report a call-out and demand increase across two sites; inspect ranked source-backed options and policy refusals, collect replies, approve one exact diff and observe closed offers, delivered/acknowledged changes and payroll draft. Miss the replacement's clock-in and require reopened coverage escalation; revoke agent authority and prove outreach stops.

- **TRADEOFF-001 — Explain cost fairness and service alternatives.** The planning agent compares candidate schedules using POS demand, service requirements, effective wage/policy data, employee preferences and the FAIR-001 fairness measures. It shows USD cost, overtime, missed preferences, FAIR-001 values, coverage/service risk and uncertainty for each alternative with source facts. Managers choose objectives and approve exceptions; protected traits are excluded from ranking and the agent cannot waive required qualifications or breaks. **Acceptance evidence:** Ask why a cheaper roster gives worse coverage or a wider closing-shift spread; inspect alternatives, calculation inputs and missing data, raise the fairness objective and see the resulting diff lower the FAIR-001 undesirable-shift spread with its cost difference shown. No proposal publishes without human decision and an impossible constraint stays visible.

- **CUTOFF-001 — Resolve payroll exceptions before the cutoff.** The payroll agent monitors actual punches, breaks, approved revisions, mapping failures and provider acceptance/application against the pay-period cutoff. It explains discrepancies in plain language, requests factual corrections from employees and follows up with accountable managers until resolved. Workers confirm facts, managers approve exact time and payroll administrators alone authorize submission/off-cycle treatment; agent reasoning cannot invent hours, hide an exception or call accepted time paid. **Acceptance evidence:** Approach payroll cutoff with a missed punch, break exception, unmapped employee and partial provider rejection. Follow agent requests through employee confirmation, manager approval and administrator submission, inspect provider readback and remaining unknowns, then handle a later correction without rewriting paid history.

- **LEARN-001 — Learn from forecast errors and unresolved outcomes.** The agent compares forecast demand, published staffing, actual attendance, sales/service outcomes, FAIR-001 measures and unresolved exceptions, attributing known error sources and retaining uncertainty. It proposes revised demand assumptions, offer timing and objective weights (including FAIR-001 fairness weights) with a measured comparison and versioned explanation. Managers approve model/policy changes; protected traits, unobserved work and unresolved payroll are never imputed as success, and reverting a model never alters historical records. **Acceptance evidence:** After several comparable service periods, inspect forecast-versus-actual errors including a no-show and stale POS interval, review a proposed assumption change and its holdout comparison, approve or decline it and revert if needed. Unobserved outcomes remain unknown in learning and reporting.
